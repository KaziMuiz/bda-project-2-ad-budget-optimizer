Why this split

  • Diminishing returns: the more you spend on one platform, the less each extra rupee earns. Google saturates fastest.
  • Google is held at 50%. Its next rupee still earns the most (Rs. 3.44), so without the limit it would get more.
  • Facebook and Instagram earn the same Rs. 2.83 from the next rupee, a sign of a balanced split.
  • Tested on unseen months: learned from January to August, predicted September to December within 0.5% to 12.7%.

Limits

Read this first

  • All data is simulated. The results show how the method works, not how real companies perform.
  • The engine is judged by its own model. The unseen-months test shows the model is reasonable, but it is not a guarantee.
  • One laptop, one run per test. MapReduce is a Python implementation, not Hadoop, and Spark ran in local mode.

Questions & Answers

The questions this project is most likely to raise, in plain words.

Explain it in 30 seconds

“My project answers one question: where should a company spend its next Rs. 1 million across Facebook, Instagram, Google, TikTok and YouTube. I simulated a year of ad data, calculated the marketing metrics, and compared Pandas, MapReduce and Spark on 10 thousand up to 10 million rows. Finally I built a recommendation engine that splits the budget, and I tested it on months it had not seen.”

The project

What does one row of your data mean?

One ad set: one campaign, on one day, for one audience segment (age, gender, city and device).

Why did you simulate the data?

There is no real company data, the handout asks for a simulation, and it lets us scale up to 10 million rows. The data is clearly marked as simulated, so the results show how the method works, not how real companies perform.

How is the simulation realistic?

Each platform has its own cost, click rate, buy rate and order value. Ages, cities and devices behave differently, and seasons like Ramadan, Eid, 11.11 and Black Friday raise spending and buying.

What is different about your project?

An optimized Pandas version to show what is really slow, memory measured as well as time, a city-by-platform heatmap, an engine that handles diminishing returns, a test on unseen months, and two simple plans to compare against.

Metrics

What are CTR, CPC, CPA, conversion rate and ROAS?

CTR is the share of viewers who click. CPC is the cost of one click. CPA is the cost of one customer. Conversion rate is the share of clickers who buy. ROAS is the revenue earned per Rs. 1 of ads.

Why do you add up first and then divide?

Averaging row-level ratios gives a row with 100 views the same weight as a row with 100,000. Dividing the totals is correct.

Which platform is best?

Google has the best ROAS (4.03). TikTok has the cheapest clicks (Rs. 8), but only 0.39% of clickers buy, so its ROAS is low. Clicks are not sales.

Big data test

What did you find?

Pandas is fastest up to about 100K rows. From about 1 million rows, Spark and MapReduce win. At 10 million rows Spark took 4.43 s and plain Pandas 23.19 s, about 5 times slower.

Why is Pandas faster on small data?

Spark and MapReduce have a startup cost. The Spark engine took about 31 s to start and used about 450 MB before reading any data, and MapReduce needs about a second to start its workers.

Explain MapReduce in simple words.

Cut the file into pieces. Each worker counts its own piece (map), results with the same platform are collected together (shuffle), and one step adds them up (reduce). It is like several people counting ballot boxes and one person adding the totals.

Is it real Hadoop, and is Spark on a cluster?

No to both. Real Hadoop needs Java and a cluster setup, so the MapReduce method is written in Python on all CPU cores. Spark runs in local mode, where the cores of one laptop act as the workers.

How do you know all tools give the right answer?

A comparison script checks that the total revenue is the same for every tool at every size, and it prints “ALL TOOLS AGREE”.

Recommendation engine

How does the engine decide the split?

It learns how revenue grows with spend on each platform, then searches all splits for the one with the highest expected revenue. At the best split, the next rupee earns about the same on every platform.

What are diminishing returns?

The first rupees on a platform earn more than later ones. We measure this with the curve revenue = a × spend^β. A lower β means the platform saturates faster, and Google has the lowest (0.72).

Why not put everything into Google?

Each extra rupee in Google earns less than the one before. At 50% of the budget its next rupee earns Rs. 3.44, and more money would lower that. The 5% minimum and 50% maximum are business rules we chose, and other limits would give another split.

How reliable are the curves?

They were learned from January to August and used to predict September to December, which the engine had never seen. The errors were between 0.5% and 12.7%, and the largest was YouTube, which has the fewest rows.

Is the +20.2% revenue real?

It is the model’s own prediction on simulated data. The engine wins by design under its own model, so it is not a guarantee. The unseen-months test is what shows the model is reasonable.

What would you do with real data?

Replace the CSV with the company’s export. The scripts stay the same, and the curves and the unseen-months check are repeated on the real numbers.
